How to tell them apart

  • parser: the shared parser reads every date month first. A day-first tenant’s date comes out with day and month swapped (7 April becomes 4 July). Every way in: app, email and the booking-v2 form. The queued fix is for this one.
  • hint: the booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People type what the hint says, and the form reads it the tenant’s way, day first. The swap looks the same as parser, but it only comes in through the v3 form. The parser fix does not touch it: the form hint has to change.
  • one-day: no swap at all. The date moves by one day (the invite says Tuesday 12 May, the calendar Monday 11 May), two clients in different time zones get different days for the same meeting, or an invite arrives before the booking was made. That points at time zones, not date order. The desk already noted these reports as one-day moves, and the #4471 page still counts them under parser.

Which problem a report belongs to depends on its tenant, its way in and what it describes. The intake tag does not decide it, because it is a guess from the reporter’s browser. The column “intake tag differs” shows how often that guess was wrong.

Why they were merged

All three came in under the same subject lines (“Date is wrong”, even “Month and day swapped?” on a report where nothing was swapped). Decision 0001 merges on subject alone, so the subject never told them apart. Only the tenant, the way in and the body do.

Lessons

  • A big ticket can hold more than one bug. Before marking it known, group it by what a fix would have to change, not by subject.
  • Count each report once: the export repeats rows across pages.
